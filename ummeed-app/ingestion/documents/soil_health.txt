Building and Maintaining Soil Health

Healthy soil is the foundation of a productive farm, and its condition depends on more than just the nutrients added through fertilizer. Soil texture, organic matter content, and microbial activity all determine how well a field can support crop growth season after season.

Getting a soil test done once a year, ideally before the sowing season, is the single most useful step a farmer can take. A basic soil test reports pH, and levels of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium — the three primary nutrients most crops need in the largest quantities. Soil that is too acidic, typically below a pH of six, limits nutrient uptake even when fertilizer is applied generously, and may need lime treatment to correct.

Organic matter improves soil structure, water retention, and nutrient availability all at once. Adding farmyard manure, compost, or crop residue back into the soil after harvest builds organic matter over multiple seasons. Green manuring — growing a fast-cycle legume crop like dhaincha or sunhemp and plowing it back into the soil before it flowers — is an effective way to add nitrogen naturally, since legume roots host bacteria that fix nitrogen from the air.

Excessive tillage breaks down soil structure over time and accelerates the loss of organic matter through oxidation. Reduced tillage or no-till practices, where the soil is disturbed as little as possible between crops, help preserve soil structure and the network of fungi and bacteria that support healthy root growth.

Salinity is a growing concern in areas with heavy groundwater irrigation and poor drainage. A white crust on the soil surface after irrigation is often a visible sign of salt buildup. Improving field drainage, choosing salt-tolerant crop varieties, and periodically flushing the field with fresh water can help manage salinity over time.

Crop rotation benefits soil health as much as pest control. Alternating deep-rooted and shallow-rooted crops improves soil structure at different depths, and rotating between nutrient-demanding crops like maize and nitrogen-fixing legumes helps maintain soil fertility without relying entirely on chemical fertilizer.
